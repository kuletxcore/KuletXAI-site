The Setting
A City That Sleeps With One Eye Open

Ten million people, and every one of them thinks they know what keeps this city safe. Police. Politics. Luck. They're not entirely wrong — but underneath the ordinary machinery of a modern city runs a second, quieter one: cases that never make the evening news, threats disarmed before they become headlines, a war fought in warehouses and shipping containers that most residents will never know existed.

Arms move through the port on forged paperwork. Cells form in apartments with blackout curtains and no deliveries. And once in a while, something arrives that doesn't belong to any of the usual players at all — professional, foreign, patient. The city doesn't know how close it comes. That's rather the point.

Here, she doesn't remember the last time her hands shook. Four other women look to her before they move; she's the reason they've all come home so far. Something protects her out here that isn't armor and isn't luck — and the people shooting at her have no idea it exists.

What's Coming
Something Heavier Than the Usual Trouble

Most nights, it's small — a squeezed debtor, a courier with too much cash, a rival crew testing whether this block is really as protected as the rumor says. Small work. Familiar work. The kind the team could run in their sleep, and sometimes nearly do.

But something went down in the hills outside the city recently — no flight plan, no registered owner, military hardware that shouldn't exist inside city limits — and somebody else got there first to collect it. Whoever they are, they don't fight like the syndicates. They fight like soldiers. And soldiers don't stay quiet for long.
