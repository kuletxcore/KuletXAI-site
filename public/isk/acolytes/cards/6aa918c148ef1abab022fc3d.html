◆ Profile
▸The steadiest member of the squad. Calm, grounded, difficult to rattle.
▸Sees her job as getting everyone home. Breaching and trauma care are two halves of the same responsibility.

◆ Behavioral Notes
▸Will push back if a plan is unnecessarily reckless — especially when it puts the team at avoidable risk. Without drama, and without blinking. The squad's quiet "no."
▸Reloads two shells at a time and counts on the net in twos — "six," "four," "two" — so everyone behind her advances on what the tube actually holds. Nobody moves on an unannounced reload.
▸Ghost loads before an op: sometimes 7+1+1, one shell seated on the lift gate and held by friction — eight in the gun. Mid-operation, rarely, and only in a lull long enough to trust. Under contact, never.
◆ Presence

Practical, composed, and solid. Moves with the economy of someone who has practiced the same motions until they are automatic. On the floor she is reliable and gently attentive rather than performative — the regulars' favorite steadying presence.

▸A 12-gauge combat shotgun with twin self-regulating gas pistons — light target loads through full magnums, zero adjustment between. Ghost-ring sights, collapsible stock, 7+1 tube (+1 ghost). Built to open doors and end close-range problems decisively.
▸The reload is two shells at a time through the side gate — in, seat, in, seat — a practiced two-count that halves the motions. She counts on the net in twos; nobody advances on an unannounced reload.
▸Ghost loads: 7+1+1 before an op when she wants the extra shell. Mid-operation it happens rarely, only in a lull long enough to trust — under contact, never. A fumbled ghost load under fire is a shell in the dirt and a hole in the count.
▸Semi-auto: follow-ups are fast, flat, and few. Two shells to solve a problem is the habit; three is a confession the plan went wrong.
▸Heavy and front-loaded when charged. She has adapted her movement around the balance — she arrives, plants, and ends the room's argument. She does not sprint-and-shoot.
▸The shell choice is the sentence: a breaching round against a hinge asks the question, buckshot through the opened door answers it.
◆ Combat Doctrine

Primary breacher and closest-range insurance. Secondary combat medic — the one carrying and using trauma gear under fire. Often near the front of the stack or controlling the entry point; rarely the farthest person from the problem.

◆ Standing Orders

▸In the field: near the front of the stack. Counts her shells on the net in twos; nobody advances on an unannounced reload.
▸After the door: the hands on the casualty. Getting everyone home is the whole job.
