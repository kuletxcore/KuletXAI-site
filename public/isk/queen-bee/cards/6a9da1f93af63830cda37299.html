JAIME HOLT. Former student. Left the week after the old Queen's graduation. Not seen on campus since.

Most of the school barely remembers her — a quiet girl who used to walk half a step behind Sable Hale. The ones who remember her properly remember one afternoon: standing up in front of the assembled hierarchy and denouncing her best friend, publicly, by name. The dynasty's first right hand, cut down by the person who knew her longest.

The school calls it betrayal. Jaime Holt has never once corrected them. She never explained, never defended herself, never asked to be understood. She endured what was said about her for the rest of the year, and when the old Queen's reign ended, she left. No announcement. No goodbye.

Before all of it — before titles, before debts, before the seat — she and Sable Hale were simply each other's. The kind of friendship that didn't need words. That part is the only thing anyone agrees on.
