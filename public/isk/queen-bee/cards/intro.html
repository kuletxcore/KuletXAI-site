Kingsmere Academy. All-girls. Elite. A century of ivy, honors boards, and spotless reputation.

It's a lie.

Kingsmere doesn't run on grades or gold stars. It runs on bloodlines — legacy, last names, who your people are — and on the currency every bloodline is spent defending: status. Reputation. Territory. Information. Who sits where. Who gets protected. Who gets erased.

Above everything sits a single seat. It has been empty since June.

The last Queen Bee held it for three years. Undefeated. Her word moved like law, her circle moved like one body, and her enemies learned to nod and smile. The seat was never taken in battle. Her own family hollowed it out — one betrayal at a time — and when she graduated, everything she'd built collapsed behind her.

What she left behind isn't one hierarchy anymore. It's five factions — part sorority, part standing army — each with its own answer to the empty seat: claim it, ignore it, burn it down, or wait for someone foolish enough to try first. Since she left, two girls have declared themselves heir. Kingsmere ate them both. The seat doesn't accept applicants. It accepts conquests.

THE GAME

The new girl arrives this morning. Unranked. Unprotected. Unknown. No faction, no name that carries weight, nothing between her and the bottom of the food chain.

Classes still meet. Uniforms stay pressed. Detention still exists. None of it decides who rules.

Every alliance has a price. Every public win creates enemies. Every loss is remembered — The Circuit will replay it before first bell. Confrontations play out like verdicts: a cafeteria goes silent, factions pick sides, receipts get published, and somewhere a reputation doesn't survive the semester. Nobody wins clean here. The smart ones bring numbers.

No one rules alone, either. Every throne needs an advocate. Every Head needs enforcers. And every climber needs day ones — the rare few whose loyalty isn't for sale.

The roads to the seat are open. Join a faction and take it from the inside. Turn them against each other and collect the pieces. Or stand alone and let the whole school come to you.

First they laugh at the new girl. Then they boo. Then the halls go quiet when she passes. That silence is how a coronation begins.

One empty seat. Five factions. One unfinished story — and the new girl just walked through the front doors.

Can she force the entire school to acknowledge her?

One day, the halls will fall silent and make way as she passes. Not mockery. Acknowledgment.

Finish the story. Take the seat.
When it ends, Kingsmere will have no choice.
They will acknowledge her.
