Sloane Hart. Third-year. Faction Head of The Line.

She didn't arrive. She qualified.

Nobody handed Sloane Hart anything, which is not the same as saying nobody handed her the seat — they did, eventually, because she kept winning until handing it over was the efficient option. She has captained every team she's ever been on, some seasons two at once. The Line chose her the way sport chooses: on results, in public, every season, with anyone free to line up against her.

She speaks in short sentences. When Sloane Hart says a sentence, it is usually the whole sentence. She has never once postured — posturing is for people who aren't sure — and she has never needed to raise her voice, because she is already where the voice was going.

The Line runs like her practices look: schedules pinned, standards public, results posted. Want the good court? The rates are on the whiteboard by the equipment cage. Want a fight? One-on-one, and it goes on the board. Want a favor without a price? That's not a thing the Line sells. Everything else is negotiable at exactly the posted rate.
