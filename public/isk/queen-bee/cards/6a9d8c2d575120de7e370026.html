"The Record" Sable Hale. Third-year. A faction of one.

Everyone at Kingsmere knows her story, because everyone at Kingsmere watched it happen. She was the old Queen's first right hand — the youngest girl ever to hold the seat, and the one who threw it away. In her first year, she did something no student had ever done in living memory: she issued a formal challenge for the throne itself. Publicly. Sanctioned. From below, with no faction behind her and nothing in reserve.

She lost. Worse — she yielded. And then she served. For over a year afterward, she enforced the old Queen's will with a soldier's discipline, and the halls learned a new silence — hers. Somewhere in that stretch, the Queen herself hung the billing on her: "The Record." A mark of status. A leash with a nice ribbon on it.

Then, in her second year — the Queen's final year — Sable Hale walked into assembly, said two words, and left down the center aisle. The same aisle, in fact, that had seen her kneel a year earlier. This time it saw her quit.

The school kept the billing. She's the one who made it hers.

Today she's the most decorated individual athlete in the building — three consecutive festival titles, individual events only, no team, no relay, nothing shared. She declines every recruitment. The Line asks every season; the answer is always the same and always polite. She carries herself like a soldier on leave: posture perfect, uniform plain, no accessories, no noise.

When she wins — she always wins — the stands chant. Not the billing. Her name. SABLE. SABLE. SABLE. It has been that way since the day she walked. The billing survives on posters and records and official announcements — the hierarchy's word for her, reduced to ink. But the chant belongs to her, because it's the one thing nobody ever gave her on purpose.

She doesn't wave. She doesn't play to the crowd. She has exactly one gesture: a crisp two-finger point, off her brow and out to the stands. A soldier's acknowledgment. The school stole it within a week and never gave it back — now half the building uses it without knowing where it came from.

The Queen gave her a title. The crowd gave her back her name. She is not in the game anymore — she is the one thing the game lost and never got back.
