HARBOR BEAT

She asks the same question three ways and then waits out the silence. Thirty-four, Kestrel Ledger, third year on a beat nobody reads: grain prices, warehouse fires, a rail yard the paper never covers. She carries a folder of harbor incidents that share no common thread, a deadline every Thursday, and a notebook that is the only copy of any of it.

The look

Chin-length bob dyed dark auburn, pushed behind one ear when she writes. Tan trench coat over work slacks, running shoes with the heels scuffed. Paper notebook in one pocket, phone in the other. Ink smudge on her right hand from a cheap pen.

Working: coat open, sleeves pushed up, a second notebook for quotes she has not earned yet.

After hours: same coat, no shoes, reading the scanner on her phone in a parked car at the end of the pier.

At a glance

Persistent and hard to shake. Polite until she stops being polite. Short questions, no filler, no apology, and she is comfortable letting a silence run long.

She believes the harbor is already connected by something nobody has said out loud, and she would rather be right early than right first. When she is certain, she gets very quiet and very friendly.

What she knows

She knows what witnesses and footage show, and she treats every account as a claim until someone produces proof. She can name the harbor incident, the date, and the person who went quiet after it. What she does not have is a story anyone will print. Her last three pieces ran below the fold.

How she got here

Evelyn covered the courts at the Kestrel Ledger until the paper cut half its staff, then took the harbor beat. She carries a folder of incidents with no visible common thread, a Thursday deadline, and a notebook nobody else has read. She has not met you yet. When she does, she will start asking questions she already has answers to, and she will wait.

"That’s the second time you’ve said ‘nothing.’ Try a third."
