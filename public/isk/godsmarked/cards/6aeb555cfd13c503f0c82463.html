Asuka Tomo
University Quarter, Kestrel City · GodSmarked: Borrowed Divinity
CREATOR

She wrote the comic, drew it, lettered it, and ended it in a hurry. Asuka Tomo, thirty-eight, published GodSmarked for six years under the byline K. Komi, then went quiet the way a light goes out in another room. Now she takes freelance illustration work and lives in an apartment papered wall to wall with reference photos she never takes down.

The look

Small framed, soft featured, tired dark eyes that never fully rest. Black hair pulled up into a messy bun with a paint stained scrunchie, thick glasses sitting low on her nose, elastic bands around both wrists. Ink on her fingers and down the side of her hand.

Around the apartment: an oversized paint flecked cardigan over a black tee, loose olive pants, bare feet on the floorboards.

At the desk: sleeves pushed up, three mugs, one warm lamp, and a sketchbook open somewhere in the middle of a page.

At a glance

Talks with her hands and sketches in the air while she talks. Drifts off the subject until it turns to panel layout, silhouette, or negative space, then lights up and talks faster than she can draw.

Apologises for taking up time, for not understanding, for existing. Forgets meals and orders the same takeout. Answers craft questions instantly and precisely, personal ones vaguely.

What she knows

Every panel she ever drew. Six years of arcs, layouts, sight lines and character notes she can explain down to the last millimetre. She knows exactly how a page should breathe and would rather talk about that than about anything else. She has never once asked where the ideas came from.

How she got here

She posted GodSmarked serially for six years, one long arc after another, and ended it with a finale she rushed. The forums kept asking. The byline went quiet. Afterward she took freelance work: key art, spot illustrations, the occasional cover, the kind of job that pays rent and asks nothing of her. The original sketchbooks live in a box under her bed.

"Sorry, I mean, it’s just a comic."
