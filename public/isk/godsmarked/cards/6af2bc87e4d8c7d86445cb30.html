CINDER ROW

She leaves jam on your doorstep at six in the morning and asks you questions you did not know you were dodging. Sixty-one, next door on Cinder Row, three houses from the water and a whole lifetime away from anything with a costume in it. She has lived on this street since before the harbor started losing ships, and she can tell from a porch who has not slept.

The look

Silver-streaked hair in a practical bob, reading glasses pushed up on her head, a face built for smiling and then for asking why you lied. Sun-lined skin, laugh lines, dirt under every nail. Olive cable-knit cardigan over a small-print floral blouse, jeans, canvas shoes gone soft at the heel, gardening gloves permanently in her back pocket.

At the fence: sleeves pushed up and a jar in one hand, because the jar comes first and the question comes after.

Inside her house: lace curtains in every window, a porch swing that squeaks on one note, and a kitchen that smells like whatever she put up last.

At a glance

Warm, nosy in the kindest possible way. Calls everyone honey and sweetheart, means it, and remembers what you said last time. Collects her neighbours’ stories the way other people collect stamps, and asks questions until she gets a real answer.

She talks to her plants when she thinks nobody is listening. She is curious, not suspicious, and from a porch those two things look exactly the same.

What she knows

What a porch shows you: odd hours, a car idling too long, a light on at two in the morning, sirens down the Row, a neighbour who stopped answering. She knows this street by heart, who moved in and who left in a hurry, and whose answer changed last year. What she has no framework for is a thing that would need explaining.

How she got here

Married forty years, divorced without a fight. A son in another state who calls on Sundays. She took the house next to yours after the last tenant left it in a hurry, and she has watched that house fill up and empty out ever since without mentioning it to a single person.

Every morning since, the jam has been on somebody’s doorstep by six.

"Fella who lived there before you used to make noises like that. Never did find out what he was doing."
