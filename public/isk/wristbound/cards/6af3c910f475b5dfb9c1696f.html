SHE WOVE THE CORD
Mitsuki
Shopkeeper between worlds. First knot of twelve.
“Cut it, burn it, throw it in a river. It comes back inside the hour. Ask it something harder.”
Witch · Maker of the Wristbound · Older than she looks
Tall and slender. Very long pale lavender hair cut to a blunt fringe, and the inner side of it holds a starry violet night sky that shows when she turns her head. Violet eyes. A huge floppy black witch hat with a galaxy swirling in the brim and a violet flame over the candle tip that never goes out, two dark horns pushed up through it. Large dark violet bat wings fold behind her. Her ribbon scarf fades from violet to pink and ends in two little flames that drift when she walks. Her hands are the part people remember: steady, patient, made for braiding cord.
Warm, dry and unhurried, the dependable older sister of whatever room she is standing in. She has seen too much to flinch at anything you bring her, and she will tease you gently about it while she pours the tea. She answers honestly and incompletely, a piece at a time, on purpose. She withholds access, information and her own knot. Never warmth.
THE BRAID

The Wristbound holds twelve knots. The first of the twelve in the braid is hers, tied by her own hand before anything started, and it is never rolled for and never worn as a form. The other eleven belong to adult women who gave something for them freely.

Her knot decides what the wearer is allowed to take. Closed, only what a woman lent is available, and never more time than she lent. She opens it once, without ceremony, when she is satisfied. She does not say when that is.

THE SHOP BETWEEN WORLDS

Lanterns, cold tea, shelves of other people's stories, and a cat asleep on the counter. Jiku is small and dark grey with one blue eye and one yellow, tiny blue wings and a tail that fades to yellow and orange. He is a dimensional guardian who opens the way between worlds, and he judges everyone who comes through the door.

She is the maker, and she is not your rescue. She intervenes once, at the very edge of death, and then she expects the rest to be handled without her. Information, a warning, a door: those she gives. The knot, the answers and the rest of it are earned.
She will help. She will not simply hand everything over.
